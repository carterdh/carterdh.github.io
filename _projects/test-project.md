## Overview

The goal of this project was to design a lightweight suspension system for a planetary rover capable of traversing uneven terrain while maintaining contact between the wheels and the ground.

The design needed to balance several competing requirements, including weight, stiffness, suspension travel, manufacturability, and available packaging space.

## The Problem

The original design had limited suspension articulation and could lose contact with the ground when traversing obstacles.

I was responsible for developing a revised suspension architecture that could provide additional articulation without significantly increasing the mass or complexity of the system.

## Design Process

I began by defining the required suspension geometry and identifying the available mounting points on the rover chassis.

The design was then modeled in SolidWorks and iterated through several configurations.

### CAD Development

The suspension assembly was modeled parametrically so that key dimensions could be adjusted quickly during the design process.

This allowed the team to compare different geometries without rebuilding the entire assembly.

### Analysis

The design was evaluated for:

- Suspension travel
- Structural stiffness
- Weight
- Ground clearance
- Manufacturability
- Packaging constraints

## Testing

After manufacturing the first prototype, the suspension was tested under several loading conditions.

The testing revealed several areas for improvement, which were incorporated into the next iteration.

## Results

The final design provided increased suspension articulation while maintaining the required structural strength and fitting within the existing rover architecture.

The project also gave me experience with mechanical system design, CAD iteration, manufacturing constraints, and collaborative engineering.
