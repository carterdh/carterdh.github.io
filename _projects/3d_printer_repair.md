## Overview

I’ve noticed through my time at Terrapin Works that employees often feel timid when they are tasked with repairing a printer (a crucial part of their job). There is training in place for new employees, but it is not comprehensive and employees often forget bits of it. In order to remedy this issue, I set out to create a series of flowcharts to guide employees through the repair process. There is a consistent style to each of the guides so that users will not get confused, and I tested it with multiple new employees at Terrapin Works to gauge its success. All employees returned their printers to working order with the help of this document.

This work highlights my ability to think like an engineer beyond just solving a technical problem. I identified a gap between formal training and real-world use, then designed a tool that reduces reliance on memory and individual confidence. The document prioritizes robustness: it works even when the user is stressed, unsure, or unfamiliar with the system. I treated the flowcharts as an engineered system, considering failure modes, user behavior, and consistency across guides. This kind of reliability-focused thinking is critical in engineering environments where small mistakes can lead to downtime, wasted resources, or safety issues, and it reflects how I approach design problems in general.
