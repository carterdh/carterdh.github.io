## Overview

At Terrapin Works, I was tasked with turning a customer’s clay-sculpted coin face into a fully 3D-printable design with a custom back. Scanning the clay model was straightforward, but producing a usable coin required complex mesh editing in Blender, which I had very little experience with. 

## Outcome

Over the course of about a week and a half, I closed that skills gap by teaching myself Blender through exploration and targeted tutorials, with an emphasis on efficiently handling high-polygon scan data. The main challenge was performance: 3D scans are extremely dense, making direct editing slow and inefficient. I solved this by developing a workflow that minimized interaction with the raw scan, designing the coin back in a separate file and switching between files to ensure stylistic consistency before merging everything into a final model.

The scope expanded when the customer requested wooden versions of the coin. To support this, I generated a height map to translate the 3D model into grayscale depth data for laser cutting, which produced promising results in initial test cuts. In the end, the customer was very satisfied with both the 3D-printed and laser-cut deliverables. The project not only demonstrated my ability to learn new software quickly but also gave me practical strategies for working with complex models under tight technical constraints.
